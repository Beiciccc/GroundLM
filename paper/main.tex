\begin{abstract}
We provide a confound-controlled methodology and a released ``wrong-question''
diagnostic for internals-based, reference-free hallucination detection, which treats
\emph{context-faithfulness} (is an answer entailed by the context?) as a linear
residual-stream direction. In the standard extractive setup, faithfulness is almost
perfectly correlated with \emph{lexical overlap} ($r{=}0.99$), so an apparent
``grounding probe'' may be a string-matching detector. Our matched
\textsc{Support}$\times$\textsc{Overlap} construction \emph{partially} decouples the two
(halving the confound to $r{=}0.50$), and the diagnostic fools an off-the-shelf NLI
model. Across four instruction-tuned $7$--$9$B families: (i)~within the
overlap-controlled stratum a support contrast is linearly decodable, but a
\emph{no-context ablation} shows it reflects question--answer compatibility rather than
context use, and is inseparable from factoid-correctness by construction; (ii)~it aligns
across families up to a linear map, yet answer-length, overlap, and factuality
directions align equally well, a generic matched-stimulus property, not
grounding-specific; (iii)~on the three families we could extract, the synthetic direction does not transfer
to real hallucinations, scoring below NLI, confidence, and lexical overlap on RAGTruth,
where only an in-domain probe trained on human labels recovers a usable signal.
\end{abstract}
