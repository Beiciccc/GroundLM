\begin{abstract}
We provide a confound-controlled methodology and a released
``wrong-question'' diagnostic for internals-based, reference-free hallucination
detection, which treats \emph{context-faithfulness}---is an answer entailed by the
provided context?---as a linear direction in the residual stream. In the standard
extractive setup, faithfulness is almost perfectly correlated with \emph{lexical
overlap} ($r{=}0.99$ in our data), so an apparent ``grounding probe'' may be a
string-matching detector. Our matched \textsc{Support}$\times$\textsc{Overlap}
construction \emph{partially} decouples faithfulness from overlap, halving the confound
to $r{=}0.50$, and the wrong-question diagnostic fools an off-the-shelf NLI model.
Across four instruction-tuned $7$--$9$B families, run API-free on a single A100-40G,
three findings follow. (i)~Within the overlap-controlled stratum a support contrast is
linearly decodable; a \emph{no-context ablation} shows this signal reflects
question--answer compatibility rather than use of the provided context, and it is
inseparable from factoid-correctness by construction. (ii)~The support contrast aligns
across families up to a linear map (orthogonal Procrustes), but answer-length,
lexical-overlap, and factuality directions align equally well, so the alignment is a
generic matched-stimulus property rather than grounding-specific. (iii)~The synthetic
support direction does not transfer to real hallucinations: on RAGTruth it scores below
NLI, model confidence, and lexical overlap, while an in-domain probe trained on human
labels recovers a usable signal. Together these establish a confound-controlled
methodology and a released diagnostic for separating a grounding signal from
lexical-overlap and question--answer-compatibility confounds.
\end{abstract}
