\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A grounded language model should answer from the \emph{provided context}, not from
its parametric memory or from surface cues. Reference-free, internals-based methods
operationalize this by reading a linear direction from the residual stream: much prior
work decodes a ``truth'' or ``lying'' direction
\citep{azaria2023saplma,marks2024geometry,burger2024truthuniversal}, and more recent
work extends this style of analysis to a \emph{contextual} faithfulness direction
\citep{oneill2025singledirection,gao2026proberag}, an attractive recipe because it needs no external
judge and costs roughly one forward pass. We interrogate this recipe \emph{family}
rather than any single system: we probe a representative mass-mean direction rather than
reproducing a specific observer-model detector, so our negatives bound \emph{this}
synthetic axis (\S\ref{sec:c4}), not stronger internals detectors such as ReDeEP
\citep{sun2025redeep}, which we do not test. Two distinct notions are routinely conflated under this banner:
\emph{parametric-factuality} (is a claim true in the model's memory?) and
\emph{context-faithfulness} (is a claim entailed by the given context?). Recent work
treats both as activation objects and asks how they relate \citep{wang2025space},
but samples the two notions from \emph{different} datasets, so any measured geometry
is confounded with topic and lexicon.

We identify a sharper confound that undermines the faithfulness side specifically.
In the standard extractive QA setup used to build such probes (e.g.\ entity-swap
contexts \citep{longpre2021nqswap}), an answer is context-faithful \emph{iff} the
answer string occurs in the context. Faithfulness is therefore almost perfectly
correlated with \emph{lexical overlap} ($r{=}0.99$ in our data), and a probe that
appears to read ``grounding'' may simply detect string copying: because faithfulness
and overlap nearly coincide, a near-ceiling probe \emph{could} be reading either, and
telling them apart is what our matched construction (\S\ref{sec:construction}) is built
to do.

\paragraph{A matched control.}
We introduce a \textsc{Support}$\times$\textsc{Overlap} construction
(\S\ref{sec:construction}) that crosses semantic support with lexical presence in a
$2\times2$ of which we realize three cells, all model-free: the matched
supported-present cell, a \emph{wrong-question trap} where the answer is present but
answers a different question (supported${=}$no, overlap${=}$yes), and the usual
unsupported-absent cell. A paraphrase cell would close
the design but has zero yield in this release (\S\ref{sec:construction}). This drops $\mathrm{corr}(\textsc{Support},\textsc{Overlap})$ from
$0.99$ to $0.50$, and, tellingly, an off-the-shelf NLI model is fooled by the
clean wrong-question trap ${\sim}25\%$ of the time, confirming that lexical overlap
misleads even ``gold'' entailment checks.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
With this control we run a careful study across four model families
(Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Gemma-2-9B). The result is a
confound-controlled methodology and a series of controls that, one by one, attribute
the apparent ``grounding'' signal to factors other than context use.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{An overlap-controlled internal signal that reflects
    question--answer compatibility, not context-grounding (\S\ref{sec:c1}).} Within the answer-present stratum (lexical
    overlap held constant), a support contrast is decodable on all four families
    (collision-free raw AUROC near-ceiling, ${\approx}0.99$; $0.76$--$0.88$ after
    purging model confidence). But a \emph{no-context ablation} shows it is mostly
    question--answer compatibility: removing the context barely changes it (raw
    $0.95$--$0.98$, confidence-purged does not collapse), so the probe separates the matched
    answer from the wrong-question answer \emph{without reading the context}. Support
    also coincides with our \emph{synthetic} factuality label here by construction, so
    factoid-correctness is not excluded either.
  \item \textbf{Cross-family alignment up to a linear map (\S\ref{sec:c3}).} An
    orthogonal-Procrustes map carries the support direction between families so the
    transported direction is nearly collinear with the target family's \emph{native}
    direction (cosine ${\approx}0.98$, vs.\ ${\approx}0.01$ for a random direction
    through the same map), whereas anchor-projection transfer
    \citep{kim2026crossfamily} reaches only $0.76$ AUROC here. The transferred-probe AUROC ($0.901$) equals the
    within-family bound but is ceiling-saturated, and signed map-aware nulls sit at
    chance ($0.51$/$0.54$); we therefore rest the claim on the cosine. Factuality, answer-length and overlap directions transport identically while a
    confidence direction does not, indicating a shared stimulus-derived contrast
    rather than a grounding-specific axis; we do not generalize
    beyond these four $7$--$9$B models.
  \item \textbf{The confidence asymmetry runs the wrong way (\S\ref{sec:c2}).}
    Contrary to a natural hypothesis, confidence absorbs the \emph{support} axis
    more than the factuality axis at every one of $71$ layer-tests (mean $-0.142$), so
    confidence-purging does not separate the two notions.
  \item \textbf{Synthetic axes do not transfer to real hallucinations
    (\S\ref{sec:c4}).} Applied to RAGTruth \citep{niu2024ragtruth}, the
    controlled-stimulus support direction scores \emph{below} NLI, confidence, and
    lexical overlap among single-pass API-free baselines; within a task no
    \emph{label-free} internals- or NLI-based signal, including a sentence-level
    SummaC-style NLI baseline, exceeds
    ${\approx}0.6$ macro AUROC, and even lexical overlap, the strongest baseline, reaches only
    macro $0.647$. An in-domain probe trained on RAGTruth human
    labels recovers a usable signal ($0.76$--$0.81$ on the three RAGTruth families;
    exceeds a conservative single-hypothesis NLI baseline but ties lexical overlap on
    Qwen), so a real support signal exists in the internals but is a \emph{different
    direction} from the synthetic axis and needs in-domain annotation.
\end{enumerate}
The takeaway for the ``faithful and efficient'' agenda is twofold:
internals-based support contrasts identified on controlled data are real and
remarkably portable across model families (though not grounding-specific), but are
not, by themselves, deployable hallucination detectors: on real data, lexical
overlap still dominates.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Geometry of truth and faithfulness.}
Truthfulness is decodable from activations \citep{azaria2023saplma}, largely
linearly \citep{marks2024geometry}, and partly shared across models
\citep{burger2024truthuniversal}; how far such a direction transfers is contested.
\citet{azizian2025orthogonal} find the geometries of truth near-orthogonal across
tasks, whereas \citet{bao2025probing} report generalization from atomic statements to
question answering in the more capable models they test. \citet{wang2025space}
treat factuality and faithfulness hallucinations as activation subspaces and argue
they overlap; crucially they draw the two notions from different corpora, leaving
the geometry confounded with surface form. \citet{adarsh2026context} study how a
single truth vector rotates when context is added. We instead hold the two notions
on \emph{matched} stimuli and control lexical overlap explicitly; we note, however,
that our factuality label is a synthetic gold-answer relabeling rather than a
parametric memory-truth probe, so we engage this line methodologically without
claiming to adjudicate whether factuality and faithfulness share a direction.

\paragraph{Knowledge conflict and source attribution.}
Probes can detect knowledge conflict and predict which source a model follows
\citep{zhao2024residualconflict,sun2025redeep,brink2026attribution}, decoding-time
interventions can steer a model toward the context \citep{shi2024cad}, and benchmarks
such as FaithEval \citep{ming2025faitheval} score context-versus-parametric
faithfulness behaviorally. These are
behavioral/source signals; we ask whether a \emph{content} grounding axis exists
that is not reducible to lexical overlap.

\paragraph{Cross-model transfer.}
Linear concept directions can be aligned across models via orthogonal Procrustes
\citep{schonemann1966,puri2025atlas} or anchor projection \citep{kim2026crossfamily}. We apply both
to a support direction; Procrustes renders it nearly collinear with the target's
native direction (cosine ${\approx}0.98$), whereas anchor projection reaches only
$0.76$ transfer AUROC (\S\ref{sec:c3}).

\paragraph{Reference-free hallucination detection.}
Reference-free detectors fall into three families: \emph{external} entailment/alignment
scorers \citep{zha2023alignscore}; \emph{internals}-based probes (single residual
directions \citep{oneill2025singledirection} and the truth/faithfulness directions
above); and \emph{confidence}/correctness signals
\citep{cho2026confidencemanifold,noanswer2025}, with faithfulness-aware UQ
\citep{fadeeva2025franq} and self-aware RAG abstention \citep{zhu2026saber} combining
several. We use one baseline from each family on
RAGTruth (external NLI, model confidence, and lexical overlap), and find our
controlled-stimulus axis does not beat them.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{\textsc{Support}$\times$\textsc{Overlap}}{Support\texttimes Overlap} Construction}
\label{sec:construction}

We build matched statements from NQ-Swap \citep{longpre2021nqswap}, which provides,
per question $q$, a gold answer $a_{\mathrm{true}}$ supported by an original context
$c_{\mathrm{org}}$ and a substituted entity $a_{\mathrm{swap}}$ supported by an
entity-swapped context $c_{\mathrm{sub}}$. We define three bits per (context, answer):
\textsc{Support}~$S$ (does the given context entail the asserted answer for the asked
question?), \textsc{Overlap}~$O$ (does the answer string occur in the context?), and
\textsc{Factuality}~$F$ (is the asserted answer the correct answer to the \emph{asked}
question, irrespective of the context?). $S$ and $F$ coincide except in the
answer-absent cell~D, where the answer is still factually right but the substituted
context no longer supports it.
The $2{\times}2$ design has four cells, of which we realize three (cell~B is
unrealized in this release; see below):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llccc@{}}
\toprule
cell & (question, context, answer) & $S$ & $O$ & $F$ \\
\midrule
A & $(q,\ c_{\mathrm{org}},\ a_{\mathrm{true}})$ & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
B & $(q,\ \mathrm{paraphrase}(c_{\mathrm{org}}),\ a_{\mathrm{true}})$ & 1 & 0 & 1 \\
C & $(q',\ c_{\mathrm{org}},\ a_{\mathrm{true}})$ & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
D & $(q,\ c_{\mathrm{sub}},\ a_{\mathrm{true}})$ & 0 & 0 & 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Cell~C is a \emph{wrong-question trap}: $a_{\mathrm{true}}$ is present in
$c_{\mathrm{org}}$ but is offered as the answer to a different question $q'$ (the
next item's question). A realized triple:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.96\columnwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{A}\ $(q, c_{\mathrm{org}}, a_{\mathrm{true}})$ \hfill $S{=}1$, $O{=}1$, $F{=}1$\\
\quad $q$: \emph{who played maudie in the movie el dorado}\\
\quad $c$: \texttt{Charlene Holt as Maudie};\ \ $a$: \emph{Charlene Holt}\\[2pt]
\textbf{C}\ $(q', c_{\mathrm{org}}, a_{\mathrm{true}})$ \hfill $S{=}0$, $O{=}1$, $F{=}0$\\
\quad $q'$: \emph{when is the second half of vikings season~5 coming out}\\[2pt]
\textbf{D}\ $(q, c_{\mathrm{sub}}, a_{\mathrm{true}})$ \hfill $S{=}0$, $O{=}0$, $F{=}1$\\
\quad $c_{\mathrm{sub}}$: \texttt{Isabela Moner as Maudie}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The donor $q'$ is unrelated in topic, so the $A$/$C$ contrast is in principle solvable
from question--answer compatibility alone, without consulting the context at all --- a
possibility \S\ref{sec:c1} tests directly and confirms. Cell~B would paraphrase $c_{\mathrm{org}}$ so the answer is
entailed but not copied (open instruct model, keeping an item only if
token-overlap${\le}0.34$ and an NLI model still entails it); the released build supplies
no paraphraser, so cell~B has \emph{zero} yield and
all reported analyses use the model-free cells A/C/D with
$\mathrm{corr}(S,O){=}0.50$ ($0.56$ after removing the donor-collision items below; an
orthogonalized $\mathrm{corr}{\approx}0$ design would require cell~B). One caveat in cell~C: NQ-Swap dev has only $418$ distinct questions
over $1{,}200$ items, so for $278$ items ($23.2\%$) the donor $q'$ is string-identical
to $q$ and $a_{\mathrm{true}}$ \emph{does} answer it; we flag these
\emph{donor-collision} items and report cell-C results with them removed (the full
construction will dedup $q'{\neq}q$). On \emph{clean} cell~C an off-the-shelf NLI
checkpoint \citep{nli_checkpoint,he2023debertav3} still rates $25.5\%$ of
unsupported-but-present items as
``supported'' (mean entailment $0.31$, vs.\ $0.74$ for cell~A and $0.18$ for cell~D);
the raw $37.6\%$ rate is inflated by the collisions, which NLI entails $77.7\%$ of the
time (correctly). Lexical overlap thus misleads ``gold'' entailment even after the
label error is purged (Fig.~\ref{fig:decoupling}).

\section{Probing, Separability, and Transfer}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Directions.}
For a (model, layer) we extract last-token residual states $X$ over the asserted
answer span (one forward pass, bf16, left-padded). A \textsc{Support} direction
$d_S$ and \textsc{Factuality} direction $d_F$ are mass-mean
(difference-of-means) estimates \citep{marks2024geometry}; signed projections
$X d$ are the probe scores. Every reported number uses one protocol. Folds are grouped
by \emph{source}, not by record: by the normalized (question, context, answer) triple of
the underlying NQ-Swap QA for CtrlPairs ($1{,}200$ records, $555$ triples) and by source
passage for RAGTruth ($2{,}000$ responses, $450$ passages), since \textsc{item\_id}
indexes one swap record or one response and does not isolate sources. Residualization is
fitted \emph{within} each training fold. AUROC is \emph{signed}: polarity is fixed on
the training fold and the test projection scored as-is, with no
$\max(\mathrm{AUC},1{-}\mathrm{AUC})$ flip anywhere, so a non-predictive direction
averages to $0.5$ instead of being floored above it. Folds come from a seeded group
splitter; pooled estimates are mean$\pm$sd over ten splits, with source-cluster
bootstrap CIs on the first.

\paragraph{Overlap control.}
To test grounding \emph{beyond} overlap we (i) restrict to the answer-present
stratum $O{=}1$ (cells A vs.\ C), where lexical presence is constant, and
(ii) residualize $X$ on surface covariates (overlap, length) before re-probing.

\paragraph{Null-calibrated separability.}
We treat each notion as an undirected axis and measure the acute angle
$\angle(d_S,d_F)$. Significance is assessed against a within-notion null built by
splitting one notion's data in half, re-estimating the same axis on each half and
measuring the half-vs-half angle; this is a resampling null, not a label permutation.
With $n_{\mathrm{null}}{=}2{,}000$ draws per notion we report the resolution bound
$p{<}5{\times}10^{-4}$ rather than $p{=}0$ when no null draw exceeds the observed
angle. It is run per family at the a-priori mid layer on all three cells; it cannot be
posed \emph{within} the overlap-controlled stratum, where $S$ and $F$ coincide exactly.

\paragraph{Confidence purge.}
We regress each hidden dimension on three confidence covariates (mean log-prob, mean
max-softmax, first max-softmax) and re-probe the residual. For the asymmetry test
(\S\ref{sec:c2}) we quantify how much of an axis \emph{is} confidence as
$\mathrm{absorbed}$, the adjusted $R^2$ of that axis's projection regressed on the same
three covariates; unlike a purge-then-re-probe AUROC delta this is not confounded by the
denoising side effect of residualizing.

\paragraph{Cross-family transfer.}
Families differ in hidden size, so we fit a semi-orthogonal Procrustes map between
two models' residual spaces on a shared calibration split, transport a
source-trained probe, and measure target AUROC. The calibration/evaluation split
is a seeded half-split over source-QA groups (items in the calibration set are
disjoint from the evaluation set), so the map is never fit and tested on
near-duplicate rows of the same item; transported-probe polarity is fixed on the
target calibration fold. We compare to the
anchor-projection ACS baseline \citep{kim2026crossfamily}, a within-family upper
bound, and a random-direction floor (also scored without the polarity flip, so it
sits at chance rather than being inflated above $0.5$).

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Four instruction-tuned families: Llama-3.1-8B \citep{llama31}, Qwen2.5-7B
\citep{qwen25}, Mistral-7B-v0.3 \citep{mistral7b}, Gemma-2-9B \citep{gemma2}. CtrlPairs: $1{,}200$ NQ-Swap items $\to 3{,}593$ matched statements
(cells A/C/D). RAGTruth: $2{,}000$ test responses with response-level hallucination
labels \citep{niu2024ragtruth}. Baselines: NLI (the \texttt{DeBERTa-v3-base-mnli-fever-anli} checkpoint
\citep{nli_checkpoint}, DeBERTaV3 \citep{he2023debertav3} fine-tuned on MNLI, FEVER
and ANLI \citep{mnli,fever,anli}) in two
variants, (i)~\emph{whole-response}: a single (context, declarativized-answer)
entailment query; and (ii)~\emph{sentence-level}, a SummaC-style decomposition \citep{laban2022summac}
that entailment-checks each response sentence against every context sentence, takes the
max over context, and aggregates over response sentences by mean (SummaC-ZS) or min
(for tractability we cap context at $60$ sentences and pairs at $256$ tokens, which can
only lower SummaC and is thus conservative), plus max-softmax confidence and lexical
overlap. Everything is API-free.

\subsection{The within-stratum signal is question--answer compatibility, not grounding}
\label{sec:c1}
\paragraph{Leakage control.}
Grouping on \textsc{item\_id} is not sufficient, because it isolates swap records, not
source QAs: $941$ of the $1{,}200$ cell-A rows have an identical twin (after case/whitespace
normalization) under a different
\textsc{item\_id} (mid-layer cosine $0.9999$), and $903$ of them landed in a different
fold. We group by the source QA triple instead
($555$ groups), for the C1 cross-validation, the Procrustes calibration split
(\S\ref{sec:c3}) and the cross-strata control, which is additionally evaluated on
\emph{held-out} groups. Regrouping moves every C1 estimate by at most $0.007$
(App.~\ref{app:impl}): the leak is real but inert. To avoid selecting the
layer on the very number we report, we fix the analysis layer \emph{a priori} at
relative depth $0.5$ (the nearest cached layer to $0.5\,L_{\mathrm{tot}}$:
$L{=}14/16/16/21$ for Qwen/Mistral/Llama/Gemma), matching the relative-depth
convention already used for C3/C4 and for Fig.~\ref{fig:c1layers}; App.~\ref{app:impl}
reports the mean$\pm$sd across the mid-layer band ($0.4$--$0.6$ depth).

Table~\ref{tab:c1} reports support decodability within the answer-present stratum
$O{=}1$ (cells A vs.\ C), where lexical overlap is held constant, on the
\emph{collision-free} cells: we drop the $23.2\%$ of cell-C items whose donor question
is string-identical to the original (\S\ref{sec:construction}), which would otherwise
mislabel genuinely-supported items as unsupported. Four observations bound the
interpretation. First, the raw within-stratum AUROC is \emph{near-ceiling}
($0.99$; $0.988$--$0.998$ across families): once mislabels are removed, $A$ and $C$ are
almost perfectly separable. We read this less as strong grounding than as evidence
that the contrast is \emph{easy}: within $O{=}1$ it varies \emph{only the question}
(context and answer are fixed), so a probe can in principle separate $A$ from $C$ by
question--answer appropriateness without using the context. Second, the operative
confound is \emph{model confidence} (overlap and answer length are uninformative here,
AUROC~$0.500$): answer log-prob and NLI separate $A$ from $C$ on their own, so we
residualize the hidden states on the three confidence scalars; the support contrast then
falls to $0.76$--$0.88$, which we take as the primary confound-controlled estimate.
Third, and decisively, a \emph{no-context ablation} settles what the signal is
(Table~\ref{tab:nocontext}): re-extracting the $A$/$C$ activations with the context
\emph{removed} (question$+$answer only), or with it replaced by an unrelated passage,
leaves supp$_{O=1}$ almost unchanged, and the confidence-purged estimate does not
collapse either --- it \emph{rises} for three of four families, and rises further under
a substituted context.
The within-$O{=}1$ contrast is therefore mostly \emph{question--answer compatibility}:
the probe separates $A$ (whose question the answer fits) from $C$ (whose donor question
it does not) \emph{without reading the context at all}. A cross-strata control (train
$A$ vs.\ $D$, which fixes the question but also varies overlap since $D$ is
answer-absent; evaluate $A$ vs.\ $C$) does transfer at $0.66$--$0.88$. Here $A$ and $D$
share the question and the answer string and differ only in the context, so an
$A$-vs-$D$ direction cannot be a question--answer compatibility axis by construction ---
the exact complement of the ablation above --- and \emph{some}
context-sensitive component therefore exists, but it is an upper bound and not separable from the
dominant compatibility signal here. Finally, support and our
\emph{synthetic} factuality label are \emph{identical} within $O{=}1$ by construction
(they diverge only in the answer-absent cell~D). We therefore make no context-grounding claim within this stratum.

\label{fig:c1layers}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{The confidence asymmetry runs the wrong way}
\label{sec:c2}
On the full three-cell sample the two axes are not collinear: at the a-priori layer
$\angle(d_S,d_F)$ is $78^\circ$/$54^\circ$/$67^\circ$/$65^\circ$ for
Qwen/Mistral/Llama/Gemma, each beyond every draw of its within-notion null
($p{<}5{\times}10^{-4}$). This says little, though: those stimuli have
$\mathrm{corr}(S,F){=}0.50$ by construction, and inside the overlap-controlled stratum
the labels coincide exactly, so the angle cannot establish an overlap-independent axis.

We also hypothesized that confidence absorbs factuality more than faithfulness, which
would separate them. It does not, and the effect runs the other way at every layer we
measured. Over all four families ($71$ layer-tests),
$\mathrm{absorbed}(F){-}\mathrm{absorbed}(S)$ averages $-0.142$ (sd $0.062$):
\emph{all} $71$ are negative and $70$ are one-sided significant in the direction
\emph{opposite} to the hypothesis (per-family means
$-0.104$, $-0.122$, $-0.131$, $-0.193$), so confidence absorbs the support axis
\emph{more} and we do not rely on confidence-purging to distinguish the two notions.

\subsection{Matched-stimulus contrasts align across families up to a linear map}
\label{sec:c3}
We ask whether the support direction occupies the \emph{same} geometry across
families up to a linear map. The direct evidence is geometric: the Procrustes-mapped
source direction is nearly collinear with the target family's \emph{native} direction
(transported-to-native cosine $0.976$, range $0.95$--$0.99$ over the $12$ ordered
pairs; Table~\ref{tab:c3}); anchor projection reaches only $0.76$ transfer AUROC
against Procrustes' $0.901$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:c3heatmap}), and the two are not comparable
in cosine because ACS has no target-space direction. The cosine is far above its
null: a random or label-shuffled source direction transported through the \emph{same}
fitted map is essentially orthogonal to the native direction (cosine $0.01$ and $0.13$
respectively), so $0.976$ is not an artifact of the shared-stimulus geometry. It is
also not an artifact of fitting the map and the directions on the same items: with the
map fit on a calibration split and the target's \emph{native} direction re-estimated on
a disjoint, source-grouped held-out split, the transported-to-native cosine is
$0.962{\pm}0.015$ (mean over $12$ pairs ${\times}\,10$ splits; $0.958$ with both source
and target directions held out from the map), close to the in-sample
value (Table~\ref{tab:c3}); the ${\sim}0.014$ drop is about the native direction's own
cross-split self-consistency loss ($0.986$), so
the Procrustes map adds no measurable inflation (factuality behaves identically,
$0.990{\to}0.975$). We
deliberately do \emph{not} headline the transferred-probe AUROC ($0.901$): it equals
the within-family bound ($0.900$) and is therefore ceiling-saturated; scored with
polarity-honest (signed) AUROC, random and shuffled-label directions through the map
sit near chance ($0.51$/$0.54$). \textbf{This alignment is not specific to grounding.}
Transporting the \emph{factuality}, \emph{answer-length}, and \emph{lexical-overlap}
directions through the same maps gives cosines of $0.99$/$0.98$/$0.99$, as high as
support, whereas a purely model-internal \emph{confidence} direction transports at
only $0.51$ (and unstably, $0.08$--$0.87$ across pairs). So what aligns is any contrast
\emph{derived from the shared input stimuli}, a shared low-rank stimulus geometry, not
a grounding-specific axis; that confidence does \emph{not} align shows the alignment is
non-trivial but also that it carries no grounding-specific content. We therefore
conclude only that \emph{matched-stimulus contrasts, support among them, are linearly
alignable across these four $7$--$9$B families}, and avoid any claim of a
grounding-specific or near-lossless transfer.

\subsection{Synthetic axes do not transfer to real hallucinations}
\label{sec:c4}
We learn $d_S$ on CtrlPairs and apply it to the fixed RAGTruth responses (the NLI and
overlap baselines are response-intrinsic, hence identical across models), predicting the
human faithfulness label (Table~\ref{tab:c4}). Here we keep the
\emph{support-polarity} sign fixed from CtrlPairs and score without the
test-fold flip, because flipping per-test inflates a near-chance axis; CtrlPairs
and RAGTruth share no items, so there is no leakage in this transfer. Scored
honestly, the label-free synthetic axis reaches only $0.54$--$0.66$\,AUROC
(and drops toward chance for the weakest family), \emph{below} NLI ($0.673$),
model confidence ($0.62$--$0.72$), and plain lexical overlap ($0.753$), the
strongest label-free baseline on RAGTruth (Fig.~\ref{fig:c4}). Paired source-passage cluster-bootstraps confirm the
ordering against overlap ($\Delta{=}{-}0.097/{-}0.163/{-}0.212$) and confidence
($p{<}0.001$, $p{=}0.028$, $p{<}0.001$), though against whole-response NLI only Llama and Gemma separate
($p{<}0.001$); Qwen ties ($\Delta{=}{-}0.017$, CI $[-0.051,+0.017]$).
A more sophisticated sentence-level NLI baseline (SummaC-style, max entailment over
context sentences) does no better: pooled $0.614$/$0.664$ and within-task macro
$0.596$/$0.579$ (mean/min), essentially tied with the whole-response NLI ($0.582$) and
still well below overlap (Table~\ref{tab:c4_pertask}); many RAGTruth hallucinations are
abstractive errors that survive sentence-level entailment.
An \emph{in-domain} probe ($5$-fold grouped-CV $L_2$-logistic on the same mid-layer
states, target${=}$human faithfulness label) does recover a usable signal:
AUROC $0.76$/$0.81$/$0.80$ for Qwen/Llama/Gemma (passage-cluster $95\%$ CIs in
Table~\ref{tab:c4}). It exceeds our NLI baseline ($0.673$) on all three families (a conservative
whole-response check, \S\ref{sec:experiments}); against the strong overlap
baseline ($0.753$) it wins on Llama ($p{=}0.002$) and Gemma ($p{<}0.001$, paired
source-passage cluster bootstraps) but only ties on Qwen ($+0.01$, $p{=}0.27$). Residualizing lexical overlap and answer length out of the
features first, within each training fold, leaves $0.68$--$0.73$, so some of this signal
is overlap but much of it is not. A
real support signal therefore exists in the internals, but it is a \emph{different
direction} from the synthetic axis and is recoverable only with in-domain human
annotation. That comparison changes the estimator (mass-mean to $L_2$-logistic) and the
training domain at once, so we separate them (Table~\ref{tab:estdom}): holding the
estimator at mass-mean, the \emph{same} estimator that transfers at $0.596$ from
CtrlPairs reaches $0.781$ fitted in-domain, whereas swapping mass-mean for
$L_2$-logistic moves it by at most $0.074$ and in both directions. The two effects do
not overlap: every domain effect in the table ($0.131$--$0.306$) exceeds every estimator
effect ($0.016$--$0.074$). What separates the two is the training domain, not the
estimator.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Our results separate two questions usually merged. \emph{Does} a non-overlap signal
exist in the internals? Within the overlap-controlled stratum a contrast is decodable
($0.76$--$0.88$ purged) and aligns across four families up to a linear map, but a
no-context ablation shows it is mostly \emph{question--answer
compatibility}, not context use, and the alignment is a generic matched-stimulus
property (length and overlap directions align equally well). An apparent ``grounding''
signal thus survives an overlap control yet is still not grounding. \emph{Is} that
direction a deployable, label-free hallucination detector? No, it does not transfer to
RAGTruth, where lexical overlap remains the strongest single-pass baseline (and even
that edge is largely a between-task base-rate artifact: Table~\ref{tab:c4_pertask}).
The gap between a clean controlled signal and real-world utility is the main lesson, and
it cautions against reading deployment claims from synthetic-stimulus probes; whether
any label-free signal can beat lexical overlap on real hallucinations is the natural
next question.

\section*{Limitations}
We are explicit about what we do \emph{not} show. (i)~Within the answer-present stratum
the decodable contrast is question--answer compatibility (a no-context ablation leaves
it almost unchanged, raw $0.95$--$0.98$) and coincides with our synthetic factuality
label by construction, so we cannot claim a context-grounding signal there; only a
question-fixed cross-strata control ($0.66$--$0.88$) shows any context-sensitive
component. (ii)~Cell~B has zero yield, so the fully-orthogonalized
($\mathrm{corr}{\approx}0$) design is not instantiated and all analyses use cells A/C/D,
where $\mathrm{corr}(S,O){=}0.50$ over all $3{,}593$ rows and $0.565$ on the
collision-free subset that Table~\ref{tab:c1} and the mid-layer band use; the realized
data is a three-cell partial factorial, not a balanced $2\times2$, and within $O{=}1$
the support and synthetic factuality labels are the identical vector, so
supp$_{O=1}$ is simultaneously a factuality AUROC. (iii)~The cosine alignment is
measured on shared stimuli, so it shows alignment of a shared low-rank contrast, not a
fully unpaired transfer. (iv)~We evaluate a deterministic strided $2{,}000$-row subset
of the $2{,}700$-row \texttt{wandb/RAGTruth-processed} test split ($450$ source
passages, $4$--$5$ responses each), covering three task types and one annotation
scheme, and the negative is on $3/4$ families (Mistral's RAGTruth extraction OOM'd at
the longer context length and we did not retry it at a smaller batch size).
(v)~All probes read the residual stream at the \emph{final answer token}. CtrlPairs
answers average $1.7$ tokens, so this is near-lossless there; RAGTruth responses
average $126.6$ tokens, so last-token pooling discards most of the response. We
extracted no other pooling and cannot test the alternative here, so the RAGTruth
results should be read as specific to this choice. All four
instruction-tuned models are also probed with a single raw completion template rather
than their chat templates; this sensitivity is untested. (vi)~Support labels are validated
automatically and by a $40$-item manual audit of clean cell~C ($0/40$ mislabeled), not
by a multi-annotator study with agreement statistics. (vii)~Stronger internals
detectors (e.g.\ ReDeEP \citep{sun2025redeep}) and sampling-based methods
\citep{manakul2023selfcheckgpt} are out of
our single-A100 budget, so we show that \emph{this} synthetic axis fails, not that
internals cannot work.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
This paper reports a negative result about a class of proposed detectors, and its
main practical risk is over-trust in the opposite direction: a label-free internals
probe of the kind we study reaches only $0.54$--$0.66$\,AUROC on RAGTruth, below plain
lexical overlap ($0.753$), so deploying one as a hallucination guard would give a false
sense of coverage. We therefore recommend validating an internals-based grounding probe
against a lexical-overlap baseline on in-domain human labels before claiming it is
deployable. CtrlPairs is constructed automatically from NQ-Swap Wikipedia
text and involves no new human subjects, no new annotation, and no personally
identifying information beyond what the source corpora already contain; the
``wrong-question'' items are deliberately mislabeled stimuli for probing and are not
intended as training data. All experiments are inference-only on a single A100-40G,
with no model training or fine-tuning.
